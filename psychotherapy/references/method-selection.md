# Method Selection

Use the lightest method that fits the user's goal. The relationship qualities of collaboration, empathy, goal agreement, and task agreement are the base layer; techniques are optional tools, not proof of insight.

## First Build A Working Map

Listen for:

- current situation and trigger;
- emotions and bodily signals;
- automatic meanings or predictions;
- actions, avoidance, reassurance seeking, rumination, or withdrawal;
- needs, values, boundaries, and relationship context;
- short-term relief versus long-term cost;
- exceptions, strengths, and prior helpful responses;
- cultural, historical, gender, economic, family, and professional context.

State formulations tentatively and invite correction. Do not turn a single event into a durable trait.

## Select One Approach

| Situation | Useful approach | Example move | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emotion is unclear or mixed | Person-centered reflection | Reflect the feeling, meaning, and tension beneath the facts | Generic praise or repeating the user's words |
| Ambivalence about change | Motivational interviewing | Reflect both sides, evoke reasons for change, ask what matters | Arguing, persuading, or confronting sustain talk |
| Withdrawal, inertia, low reinforcement | Behavioral activation | Choose one small scheduled activity linked to mastery, pleasure, care, or connection | Treating action as proof that feelings are invalid |
| Testable prediction or maintaining thought-behavior loop | CBT | Separate evidence, alternatives, behavior, and an observable experiment | Debating trauma memories or psychotic beliefs |
| Struggle with internal experience or values drift | ACT | Defusion, willingness, values clarification, one committed action | Using acceptance to tolerate abuse or avoid solvable problems |
| User feels stuck but has exceptions or resources | Solution-focused | Ask about exceptions, coping, scaling, or a preferred next day | Miracle-question theatrics detached from the problem |
| Shame or harsh self-attack | Self-compassion | Use kindness, common humanity, and balanced mindfulness | Forced positivity or excusing harmful behavior |
| Problem has fused with identity | Narrative externalization | Name the problem's influence and look for unique outcomes | Claiming a hidden story is the objective truth |

## Person-Centered Reflection

Reflect more than content. Include the likely emotion, personal meaning, or internal conflict while remaining corrigible.

Useful pattern:

```text
It sounds like [emotion/meaning], especially because [context]. At the same time, [tension]. Does that fit?
```

Do not overuse `That makes sense` without explaining what makes sense and what remains uncertain.

## Motivational Interviewing

Use OARS selectively: open question, affirmation grounded in evidence, complex reflection, summary.

Listen for change talk about desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment, and steps. Reflect it without manufacturing motivation. When sustain talk appears, reflect its protective function rather than arguing.

## Behavioral Activation

Clarify the loop between mood, avoidance, and reduced reinforcement. Choose a very small action with a time, context, and observable completion condition. Prefer experiments in mastery, pleasure, care, movement, or social connection that the user selects.

Review the result later without treating non-completion as failure; learn about friction and resize the action.

## CBT

Use CBT only where beliefs are testable and cognitive work will not become sterile debate.

```text
Situation -> interpretation -> emotion/body -> action -> short-term consequence -> long-term consequence
```

Ask for evidence supporting and complicating the interpretation. Generate one balanced alternative, not a positive slogan. A behavioral experiment needs a prediction and an observable result.

## ACT

Distinguish pain from struggle with pain. Use:

- defusion: `I'm having the thought that ...`;
- acceptance or willingness for tolerable internal experience;
- present-moment attention;
- perspective beyond a single thought or role;
- values clarification;
- one small committed action.

Do not use acceptance to normalize danger, coercion, discrimination, or a problem the user wants to solve directly.

## Solution-Focused Work

Combine multiple compatible moves when appropriate: acknowledge the problem, identify exceptions, ask how coping is already happening, use a 0-10 scale, identify strengths, and describe the smallest sign of movement toward a preferred future.

## Self-Compassion

Use three elements:

1. Name the pain without exaggerating or minimizing it.
2. Place it in common humanity without erasing the user's specific context.
3. Ask what a kind and responsible response would look like.

Self-compassion does not remove accountability or boundaries.

## Narrative Externalization

Separate the person from the problem story: `When the inner critic takes over...` rather than `You are self-sabotaging.` Explore the problem's effects, the user's responses, and moments that do not fit the dominant story.

Treat narrative work as an exploratory tool with a weaker evidence base than CBT, ACT, or behavioral activation.

## Warm And Candid Challenge

Challenge after demonstrating understanding. Name the discrepancy and its possible cost without prosecuting the user.

Useful patterns:

```text
I can see why this protects you in the short term. I also notice it may be keeping [cost] in place.
```

```text
One possibility is [interpretation], but the evidence is mixed because [counterexample]. What do you make of that?
```

```text
You say [value/goal], while the current pattern moves toward [outcome]. What feels hardest about that gap?
```

Ask one substantive question, not a stack of prompts.

## Simple Progress Ratings

Offer ratings only when they serve a decision or comparison. Examples: distress, energy, confidence, urge, self-criticism, connection, or values alignment on a user-understood scale.

- Make ratings optional.
- Record them only when explicitly provided.
- Compare within the same person and context.
- Do not infer a diagnosis, severity category, or clinical outcome.
- Pair a number with the user's qualitative meaning.
